LEAP 2026 opens today in Riyadh, betting the Gulf's AI story has moved past big numbers

AI & techGCCMarket data14 Aug 2026 · recent

LEAP 2026, Saudi Arabia's flagship technology and AI conference, now in its fifth edition, opens today in Riyadh and runs through 3 September, rescheduled from its original April slot due to regional disruption. Organisers expect roughly 200,000 visitors, 1,800 exhibitors, 600 startups and 1,900 investors; the prior edition (LEAP25) announced $14.9bn in AI investment on opening day alone, against a cumulative $42.4bn committed since 2022. Confirmed CX/enterprise-relevant exhibitors already pre-announced include Liferay (AI Hub for governed, data-grounded AI agents, covered 28 Aug), NTT DATA and Mannai IT. A Wamda analysis published 24 August argues the more useful signal this year isn't the headline investment total but whether commitments convert into working products: the piece frames this as watching for "integration schedules, costs and ownership" conversations rather than demos, Arabic-first products built for Arabic speakers (not merely localised Western tools) as a marker of genuine regional tech creation, and how many people in the region ship software for the first time this year. For MECSC members tracking vendor pitches this week, that's a useful filter for separating substance from investment-figure headlines.

DEWA's AI cuts customer refund processing from four days to eight minutes

AI & techUAECX3 Aug 2026 · recent

Dubai Electricity and Water Authority has automated security-deposit refunds (up to AED 4,000) with AI, cutting turnaround from four business days to roughly eight minutes, a 99% reduction, as part of its stated ambition to be "the world's first AI-powered utility." The move sits alongside DEWA's long-running virtual assistant Rammas, which has handled more than 13 million customer enquiries since 2017 and has recently been upgraded with generative AI. The practical read for MECSC members: this is backend process automation rather than a customer-facing chatbot launch, and it's a useful reminder that "AI in CX" wins for utilities and government services increasingly show up as invisible speed improvements (refunds, approvals, verifications) rather than new conversational interfaces.
