Customer experience & service

1

Saudi Cabinet approves a unified Consumer Protection Law, with fines of up to SAR 8 million and up to two years in prison

CXSaudi ArabiaPolicy11 Nov 2026 · recentConfidence: High

Saudi Arabia's Council of Ministers approved a new Consumer Protection Law on Tuesday 10 November; it takes effect 180 days after publication in the Official Gazette and replaces the Anti-Commercial Fraud Law, the E-Commerce Law and the Commercial Data Law. Violations can bring fines of up to SAR 8 million, up to two years in prison, suspension of business activities and confiscation of non-compliant products, and the law bans extra fees for particular payment methods and lets the authorities cap prices or profit margins, or require approval for price rises, in crises. Asharq Al-Awsat adds bans on fabricated reviews and false scarcity claims, a seven-day right to cancel contracts made outside business premises, and minimum warranties of two years for new products and one year for services and used products.

Why it matters: Once in force it sets new rules for Saudi retailers and mall tenants on payment surcharges, reviews, promotions, warranties and cancellations, a ready briefing topic for MECSC's customer-service members.

AI & retail technology

1

OpenAI launches ChatGPT Wallet so its agents can pay for purchases, first for some US Pro subscribers

AI & techGlobalLaunch13 Nov 2026 · in windowConfidence: Medium

OpenAI launched ChatGPT Wallet on 13 November, letting its AI agents, called dots, help users decide what to buy and then complete the purchase; it is open to a limited number of US ChatGPT Pro subscribers aged 18 and over with a US-issued Visa, Mastercard or American Express card. The card is charged with a payment token so its details are not shared with the agent or the merchant, and the user reviews the items, total and card and approves each purchase with Face ID or a fingerprint; OpenAI plans spending limits and rules by purchase category or time period. It also published a Commerce Partner Playbook for retailers that want to support agent shopping, while TechCrunch notes that Amazon bans outside agents and that DoorDash and Uber report low numbers of agent orders.

Why it matters: It moves agent shopping from product discovery (Businesses in ChatGPT, see 12 Nov) to payment, and its tokenised cards and per-purchase approval are a reference point for GCC banks, card schemes and retailers deciding whether to admit outside shopping agents.
