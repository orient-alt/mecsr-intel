Riyadh Municipality offers its first joint land investment with the private sector: a 30,000 sqm mixed-use plot on a 50-year term

DestinationsSaudi Arabia · RiyadhPolicy3 Nov 2026 · in windowConfidence: Medium

Riyadh Municipality, the Ministry of Municipalities and Housing and the National Center for Privatization and PPP have opened the expression-of-interest, qualification and proposal phase for a 30,000 sqm plot on Zaid bin Asim Street in the municipality's northern sector, Argaam reported on 3 Nov 2026. It is the municipality's first project under a joint investment model with the private sector: the partner would design, build, operate and maintain all facilities under a 50-year agreement, and the plan allows residential, commercial, office and entertainment uses. Submissions are open until 3 Feb 2027 through the NCP investor portal; no retail format or floor area has been set.

Why it matters: It opens a new route to municipal land in north Riyadh for mixed-use schemes with commercial and entertainment space, which mall developers and operators can bid for.

UAE gold jewellery demand falls 29% to 4.5 tonnes in Q3 2026, against a 17% fall worldwide

DestinationsUAEMarket data3 Nov 2026 · in windowConfidence: Medium

The World Gold Council's Gold Demand Trends: Q3 2026, published on 3 Nov 2026, puts global jewellery consumption at 313 tonnes, down 17% year on year, while total gold demand including OTC slipped 1% to 1,369 tonnes. Khaleej Times, reporting the council's data on the same day, gives UAE gold jewellery demand of 4.5 tonnes in the quarter, down 29% year on year and 19% on the second quarter, and UAE bar and coin demand of 2.9 tonnes, down 15% year on year and 46% on the quarter. The council's report page gives only the global figures, so the UAE numbers rest on Khaleej Times' reading of the data tables for now.

Why it matters: Jewellery is a core tenant category in UAE malls and gold souks, and lower volumes at high prices point to weaker trading at those units going into the Dubai Shopping Festival (4 Dec 2026 to 17 Jan 2027).

Sports Direct opens its first stores in Africa, at Sarit Centre and Galleria Mall in Nairobi

DestinationsKenya · NairobiMarket entry2 Nov 2026 · recentConfidence: High→ 2027 Directory: Check first

Hudson Group, Frasers Group's partner for the brand, has opened Sports Direct's first two African stores in Nairobi: 930 sqm at Sarit Centre and 725 sqm at Galleria Mall. The stores sell Nike, adidas, Under Armour, PUMA, New Balance and ASICS alongside Frasers Group brands such as Everlast, Slazenger, Karrimor, Jack Wills and USA Pro, and a three-day Sports Village at Galleria Mall from 30 Oct to 1 Nov 2026 marked the openings. Hudson's chief executive said it will build on this as it expands Sports Direct across Africa; the release gives no exact opening dates or further sites.

Why it matters: A major international sports chain has entered Africa through two established Nairobi malls, neither of which is in the directory yet.

OVS takes direct control of its UAE stores, led by a 2,500 sqm Dubai Mall flagship, and seeks units of 1,000 sqm or more in Saudi Arabia

DestinationsUAE · DubaiExpansion30 Oct 2026 · recentConfidence: High→ 2027 Directory: Update listing · Low

Italian fashion group OVS has set up a local subsidiary and a logistics hub and taken direct control of its UAE operations, chief executive Stefano Beraldo told Retail ME on 30 Oct 2026. Its 2,500 sqm flagship at The Dubai Mall, opened in September 2026, is the group's largest store worldwide and its eighth in the UAE; about 6,000 shoppers joined its loyalty programme in the first month, and a UAE online store is expected within one or two months. In Saudi Arabia OVS has changed partner (not named) and is now targeting stores of at least 1,000 sqm; Beraldo sees potential for dozens of new stores across the Middle East and India but gave no number or timeline.

Why it matters: A European value-fashion group running its own UAE stores and asking for larger units in Saudi Arabia points to new demand for big fashion boxes in GCC malls.

Holiday surveys: shoppers use AI to find gifts and compare prices, but most want to approve any purchase

AI & techGlobalMarket data3 Nov 2026 · in windowConfidence: Medium

Retail Dive's round-up of 2026 holiday research, published on 3 Nov 2026, finds shoppers using AI mainly at the start of the journey. Gartner's 2026 holiday report finds that 13% of shoppers already use AI tools or chatbots for holiday inspiration and about a third are very or extremely willing to use them for gift ideas; William Blair's October survey finds that most respondents would not let AI make a purchase without a review and most are unwilling to share bank or card details with it. Bank of America names comparing prices or deals as the most common reason to use AI for holiday shopping. The article gives no sample sizes or survey countries.

Why it matters: It gives Gulf retailers and malls a benchmark for AI services before the Dubai Shopping Festival: AI for discovery and price comparison is welcome, while handing checkout to an agent still meets resistance.

Retail Dive · 3 Nov 2026 ↗B

Salesforce agrees to buy Cresta, whose voice AI agents handle customer calls from start to finish

AI & techGlobalDeal / JV2 Nov 2026 · recentConfidence: High

Salesforce has signed a definitive agreement to acquire Cresta, a voice-first customer experience platform whose AI agents handle complex calls from start to finish and whose software coaches human agents in real time and analyses every interaction; Marriott, Comcast and Alaska Airlines are named customers. Salesforce will combine Cresta with Service Cloud so that each conversation is linked to the customer's history and to outcomes such as resolution, retention and revenue, and Cresta will keep supporting the contact-centre and telephony platforms its customers already use. No price was disclosed, and closing is expected in the first quarter of Salesforce's fiscal year 2028, subject to regulatory approvals. The announcement went out at 21:00 UTC on 2 Nov (01:00 on 3 Nov in the UAE), two weeks after Salesforce agreed to buy Braze.

Why it matters: Gulf retailers, mall operators, banks and telcos that run customer service on Salesforce are likely to be offered voice agents that take calls end to end, so MECSC service standards will need to cover how such agents perform and hand over to staff.

Salesforce (newsroom) · 2 Nov 2026 ↗A
