Primark opens its first Bahrain store at City Centre Bahrain on 23 September

DestinationsBahrainExpansion3 Sep 2026 · in window

Primark, via its partnership with Alshaya Group, confirmed today that its first Bahrain store will open at City Centre Bahrain on 23 September, stocking womenswear, kidswear, accessories, beauty and homeware under the retailer's "trend-led fashion, everyday essentials and unbeatable value" positioning. Opening day is planned around "entertainment, giveaways and activations designed to create a memorable shopping journey", a deliberate experience-led launch rather than a quiet stock-and-open. The announcement also confirmed that Primark's existing Mall of the Emirates store will add a second floor in November 2026 (expanding menswear and home ranges), and that further GCC entries into Kuwait and Qatar are planned for 2027. Useful for members tracking Alshaya's GCC retail-rollout playbook and how international value-fashion brands are using launch-day programming, rather than just store openings, as a customer-experience differentiator.

MOCI's Q2 2026 report posts 93–96% citizen satisfaction across digital and in-person channels

CXQatarCX1 Sep 2026 · in window

Qatar's Ministry of Commerce and Industry reported strong Q2 2026 performance across its digital-services and consumer-protection remit: 131,269 total transactions processed, 86% of them completed electronically, alongside a 93% satisfaction rate for online applications and 96% for applications handled at physical Government Services Centres. The ministry deployed 15 new digital services during the quarter, issued 6,745 new commercial registrations and registered 5,272 foreign companies. On consumer protection, of 9,511 complaints received, 7,808 concerned consumer-protection matters, with most resolved within five working days and 83,339 inspection visits completed to monitor market compliance. The gap between online (93%) and in-person (96%) satisfaction is a small but real signal for members benchmarking omnichannel government CX, the physical-channel edge suggests Qatar's Government Services Centres still out-perform the digital front door on experience quality, even as electronic completion rates climb.

1

ServiceNow and Aramco Digital sign AI-powered enterprise-transformation agreement

AI & techSaudi ArabiaCX2 Sep 2026 · in window

Signed on LEAP 2026's closing day in Riyadh, the collaboration agreement between ServiceNow and Aramco Digital aims to standardise digital workflows across Aramco's distributed network spanning 50+ countries, building "a consistent AI-enabled foundation that can scale across the ecosystem." The agreement spans four domains: AI-powered workflows (intelligent automation to cut manual processes), customer experience (unified, CRM-driven service-management standards across entities), group shared services (reducing redundancy), and ERP modernisation (retiring legacy technical debt). ServiceNow's platform, which the company says now handles over 100 billion workflows annually, provides the governance and orchestration layer. The explicit naming of "customer experience" as one of four transformation pillars, not just an efficiency play, is the notable detail for MECSC members: it's a large-scale example of a Gulf enterprise treating CX standardisation as core infrastructure work rather than a bolt-on, at a moment when most GCC AI headlines skew toward government or retail rather than enterprise B2B service management.
