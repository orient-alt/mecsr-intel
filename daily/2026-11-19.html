Majid Al Futtaim to build an AED 260m staff parking facility at Mall of the Emirates, freeing more than 1,000 shopper spaces by December 2027

DestinationsUAE · DubaiExpansion18 Nov 2026 · in windowConfidence: High

Majid Al Futtaim will invest AED 260 million in a parking facility on a 52,000 sqm plot adjacent to Mall of the Emirates, which it calls a key milestone in the mall's AED 5 billion expansion. Staff parking will move to the new facility, freeing more than 1,000 customer parking spaces across the mall's main zones, with completion scheduled for December 2027. Khalifa Bin Braik, chief executive of Majid Al Futtaim Asset Management, is quoted; neither MAF nor Gulf News gives the facility's own capacity, its contractor or new GLA and store figures for the wider expansion. The release carries an 18 Nov dateline, although MAF's media centre lists it under 12 Nov.

Why it matters: Moving staff parking off-site to free shopper capacity during a phased AED 5bn rebuild is a practical model for owners expanding busy malls on tight sites, and it changes the parking note for a listed mega mall.

talabat raises its 2026 guidance for the second time as Q3 revenue rises 18% and net income falls 15%

BrandsUAE · DubaiFinancial13 Nov 2026 · recentConfidence: High

talabat's Q3 2026 revenue rose 18% to USD 1,185 million and GMV rose 16% to USD 2,996 million (17% at constant currency), while net income fell 15% to USD 102 million and adjusted EBITDA fell 3% to USD 150 million; free cash flow rose 41% to USD 141 million (talabat release, 13 Nov). GMV in Egypt, Jordan and Iraq grew 43% to USD 736 million, 25% of the total against 20% a year earlier, while GCC GMV grew 9% to USD 2,260 million; revenue grew faster than GMV on a higher share from talabat's own grocery business, talabat mart. It raised its full-year guidance for the second time this year, to net income of USD 360 to 370 million (previously USD 325 to 355 million) and revenue growth of approximately 19% at constant currency.

Why it matters: talabat's growth in grocery and in Egypt, Jordan and Iraq bears directly on the delivery and online-grocery competition facing mall hypermarkets and food and beverage tenants.

Dubai Municipality unveils D.Mind, an AI platform that pools complaints from its call centre, app and social media and suggests fixes

CXUAE · DubaiLaunch18 Nov 2026 · in windowConfidence: Medium

Dubai Municipality unveiled D.Mind (Dubai Municipality Mind) at Emirates Towers on 18 Nov, the first day of Dubai Future Week 2026. The platform gathers complaints from the municipality's call centre, mobile app and social media accounts, including Instagram and X, runs 24/7, and passes suggested solutions to the department concerned, along with longer-term fixes for recurring problems. It draws on teams in digital transformation, GIS and customer happiness; Gulf News gave no complaint volumes, resolution times or rollout dates.

Why it matters: It joins call-centre, app and social-media complaints into one live view with root-cause fixes, a benchmark for the complaint handling of malls and retailers that MECSC members can measure themselves against.

Dubai approves a 'City AI Agent' to coordinate services across government entities, and presents 93 back-office AI agents

AI & techUAE · DubaiAI / tech18 Nov 2026 · in windowConfidence: High

Sheikh Hamdan bin Mohammed approved the City AI Agent initiative at the AI Retreat during Dubai Future Week 2026, which opened on 18 Nov and runs to 21 Nov. The agent is meant to understand people's needs, carry out tasks and coordinate procedures across government entities; no launch date or list of services was given. Dubai also presented a Government Resources AI Agents ecosystem of 93 agents for human resources, finance and procurement, expected to complete more than 34 million tasks a year, save over 812,000 working hours and cut costs by more than AED 151 million across 85 government entities.

Why it matters: A government agent that acts on residents' requests across departments raises the service standard customers will expect from malls, retailers and banks; it is a Dubai initiative, separate from the federal services transformation still due in November (watch list).

Mars research with Kantar and Synthesis expects AI assistants to handle more routine grocery shopping and stores to become destinations for discovery

AI & techUAE · DubaiReport18 Nov 2026 · in windowConfidence: Medium

Mars released research on the future of shopping on behalf of its snacking business, carried out with Kantar and Synthesis, which analysed more than 180,000 global data signals, including stakeholder and expert interviews, across four continents, with the UAE among the top contributing markets (Campaign Middle East, 18 Nov). It expects routine grocery runs to become increasingly automated over the next decade, with shopping turning into a background service managed by AI assistants, and physical stores and digital platforms to become destinations that shoppers choose for discovery and entertainment. It names three shopper demands: effortless, personal and experiential. This is brand research: the article gives no respondent count, UAE-specific statistic or fieldwork dates.

Why it matters: It argues that malls and stores will win visits through discovery and entertainment once AI handles routine purchases, a theme for mall operators' leasing and marketing plans.

Campaign Middle East · 18 Nov 2026 ↗B

EU's proposed Digital Fairness Act would give shoppers a right to human customer service and ban fake 'almost sold out' claims

AI & techGlobal · BrusselsPolicy11 Nov 2026 · recentConfidence: Medium

The European Commission presented its proposed Digital Fairness Act on 11 Nov as part of a consumer package. Its Q&A says consumers will have the right to access human customer service when problems arise in an ongoing contract and other means are not effective, and the package bans manipulative tactics such as fake claims that a product is almost sold out, requires clear disclosure of influencer marketing and makes digital subscriptions easier to cancel. Sourcing Journal (16 Nov) reports that the proposal also lets shoppers who use AI agents to buy on their behalf set and change the key parameters of those automated systems; the Commission pages read do not mention agents. It is a proposal, with no adoption date given.

Why it matters: A right to reach a human and rules for agent-led buying are reference points for GCC regulators, banks and retailers, none of which had set rules on outside AI shopping agents by 19 Nov (watch list).
