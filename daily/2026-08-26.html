HSBC picks the UAE to launch its first "immediately international" account

CXGlobalCX25 Aug 2026 · in window

HSBC UAE became the first market in HSBC's global network to launch the upgraded Premier Global Money Account, a fully integrated multi-currency account and linked Visa debit card that is international by default from the moment a customer is onboarded. Customers hold, spend, transfer and receive in 20+ currencies under a single IBAN with no HSBC fees on supported currencies, removing the usual friction of separate accounts, activation steps and FX timing. The CX read-across matters more than the product: HSBC explicitly chose the UAE ahead of every other market in its network because a majority of residents already transact in multiple currencies, and the proposition is expected to reach other HSBC markets over the next one to two years. UAE Chief Executive Mohamed Al Marzooqi tied it to "continued investment in new, state-of-the-art wealth centres", i.e. a combined digital-and-physical service model, not a digital-only play. For MECSC members, this is a clean example of a global brand treating the Gulf as its lead customer-experience market rather than a follower market.

Loyalty & Reward Co opens Dubai office as regional loyalty market heads for US$5.6bn

CXUAELeadership25 Aug 2026 · in window

The global pure-play loyalty consultancy opened a Dubai office and appointed Krishna Mohan as Loyalty Director (MENA). Mohan brings two decades of GCC loyalty and CRM leadership, he scaled Shukran Rewards at Landmark Group, launched the Blue Rewards ecosystem at Al-Futtaim, and most recently led the evolution of Nahdi's Nuhdeek programme across KSA and the UAE. The firm targets MENA at 20% of global revenue by 2028. The market framing is the useful part. Middle East loyalty is forecast to grow from US$3.4bn in 2026 to US$5.6bn by 2030 (12.8% CAGR), with the UAE reaching US$592.4m and Saudi Arabia US$1.27bn. The firm identifies five shifts relevant to mall and retail operators: rewards moving to everyday journeys (fuel, grocery, mobility, bill payment); AI-driven personalisation at scale across physical and digital channels; loyalty embedded inside national wallets and payment apps, with a warning that "programs without payment integration risk losing relevance"; large regional groups consolidating multi-brand ecosystems so members earn and redeem across grocery, malls, fashion, dining and entertainment; and e-commerce/quick-commerce platforms building their own loyalty layers to escape discounting.

Salesforce State of Commerce: UAE commerce leaders put AI first, but omnichannel still breaks

CXUAECX11 Aug 2026 · recent

Labelled Recent Background, published 11 August, outside the coverage window but directly relevant to both streams and not previously expanded in detail. Implementing or expanding AI now ranks as the number one priority for UAE commerce leaders, ahead of growing the customer base. 62% have optimised content for conversational and question-based queries, 61% improved product content quality, 58% submit data feeds directly to AI search platforms, and 31% monitor how their brands appear in AI-generated responses. The failure points are the CX story: for UAE B2B organisations, ordering channels siloed across multiple systems is the most common breakdown (44%), followed by inventory data not synchronised in real time (37%), inability to recognise customer information consistently across channels (35%) and inconsistent returns/exchanges (35%). Only 1% of UAE multichannel organisations report no significant breakdowns at all. Among UAE organisations that have unified customer data, 48% report improved AI and automation outcomes, 43% higher conversion, 43% better retention and 41% faster issue resolution.

The agentic CX scorecard changes: containment out, proven resolution in

AI & techAI / tech24 Aug 2026 · in window

CX Today's analysis of three simultaneous vendor moves, ServiceNow's Autonomous Workforce, Salesforce's Headless 360 expansion and Synthflow's voice-AI resolution push, argues that AI in customer experience has passed the point where a good conversation is enough. The new test is whether an AI agent actually completed the customer's task, operated within policy, and left a clean route to human recovery when it failed. Three points worth carrying into member conversations. First, the commercial signal is real but not proof of capability: ServiceNow reports AI annual contract value above US$1bn and a ninefold increase in agentic deployments in nine months, buyer intent, not evidence that autonomous service works consistently in complex environments. Second, workforce planning breaks: removing simple contacts leaves human agents with complaints, exceptions, sensitive cases and journeys where AI already failed once, so forecasting now needs AI completion rates, failed-action rates, transfer rates and repeat contacts as inputs. Third, Salesforce's MCP-based architecture (Data 360 exposing roughly 200 APIs, with agents inheriting existing object relationships, validation rules and permissions) is architecturally significant but inherits weaknesses too, "poorly defined permissions, fragmented customer data, inconsistent business rules... will travel with the agent." ServiceNow's Amit Zavery put the shift bluntly: "Advisory AI has run its course; enterprises need AI that senses, decides, and securely acts in accordance with organizational guardrails." Verizon and Google Cloud put agentic AI behind 28,000 customer-care reps

AI & techCX25 Aug 2026 · in window

Verizon announced a strategic partnership with Google Cloud to expand Gemini Enterprise, modernise enterprise data and scale AI across customer service, network operations, marketing and employee workflows. The conversational agent, built on Vertex AI, Gemini models and the Agent Assist panel, supports 28,000 customer-care representatives and retail stores, functioning as a "personal research assistant" that anticipates customer needs. A separate "problem solver agent" guides reps through advanced troubleshooting on complex queries rather than simply returning an answer. Verizon claims the partnership has produced "95 percent comprehensive answerability for customer inquiries". The deliberate design choice is the transferable lesson for GCC telecoms and large retailers: AI absorbs the volume of everyday interactions while human support is reserved for moments that are complicated, sensitive, or likely to damage loyalty, an assist-first model rather than a deflection-first one.
