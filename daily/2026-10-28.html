Saudi Cabinet approves a 271-article Commercial Transactions Law that replaces three older trade laws

DestinationsSaudi Arabia · RiyadhPolicy27 Oct 2026 · in windowConfidence: Medium

The Council of Ministers approved the Commercial Transactions Law at its session on Tuesday 27 Oct. According to the Ministry of Commerce, the 271-article law replaces the Commercial Law, the Commercial Papers Law and the Commercial Books Law, and brings commercial sale, supply sale, brokerage and commercial agency into one text that works alongside the Civil Transactions Law.

It lets people aged 15 and over trade with permission from a guardian, custodian or court, allows accounting books to be kept electronically and bans practices that harm competitors, with a right to go to court to stop them and claim compensation. It takes effect 180 days after publication in Umm Al-Qura. Neither report read mentions leases, franchising or e-commerce.

Why it matters: The law rewrites the basic contract and record-keeping rules for every retailer, supplier and commercial agent in the Kingdom, so members trading in Saudi Arabia will want to review agency and supply terms before it takes effect.

Saudi electricity and water tariffs change from 1 Jan 2027, and the Nitaqat pay threshold for Saudi staff rises to SAR 5,000, then SAR 6,000

DestinationsSaudi ArabiaPolicy25 Oct 2026 · recentConfidence: High→ 2027 Directory: Article idea

Saudi Energy Company said on 25 Oct that it will adjust its electricity service tariff from 1 Jan 2027 under the procedures for adjusting energy and water prices (SPA), and the National Water Company is changing its tariffs from the same date. Alongside them, King Salman ordered a SAR 500 rise in monthly allowances for civil and military employees, retirees and eligible beneficiaries, and a SAR 500 monthly payment for families on social security.

The salary a Saudi private-sector employee must earn to count fully towards Nitaqat quotas rises to SAR 5,000 six months after the changes take effect, then to SAR 6,000 twelve months later (Argaam; Asharq Al-Awsat). None of the reports read gives the new rates for commercial customers such as shops and malls.

Why it matters: Electricity and Saudi staff are two of the largest running costs for Saudi malls and retailers, so members need the commercial tariff tables for 2027 service charges and should budget for the higher Nitaqat pay floors.

Aldar's Q3 2026 revenue rises 16% to Dh9.3bn while development sales fall 34% to Dh6.8bn

MembersUAE · Abu DhabiFinancial27 Oct 2026 · in windowMECSR memberConfidence: High

Aldar Properties reported its third-quarter 2026 results on 27 Oct: revenue rose 16% year on year to Dh9.3bn (The National gives 16.4% for revenue and rental income), while group development sales fell 34% to Dh6.8bn and UAE sales fell 45% to Dh5bn. The group revenue backlog stood at Dh71.6bn at the end of September, and Aldar launched seven projects in the quarter, taking this year's launches to 12.

Neither report gives figures for Aldar's malls or investment properties, and the two outlets report net profit on different bases (Q3 attributable profit in one, nine-month profit in the other), so profit is left out until Aldar's own release can be read.

Why it matters: These are the first Q3 2026 results from a MECSR member: revenue is still growing on the backlog while new UAE sales have slowed sharply, a pattern to watch as the other developer-landlords report.

Seef Properties brings AI avatars, web chat and voice service to its Bahrain malls with Total CX

CXBahrainCX26 Oct 2026 · recentMECSR memberConfidence: Medium→ 2027 Directory: Article idea

Seef Properties has partnered with Total CX to add interactive AI avatars to its shopping malls in Bahrain, combined with web chat and voice, so visitors can find stores, get mall information, give feedback and request services. Enquiries that need a person are passed to customer service teams, and the system analyses visitors' questions, feedback and service use to improve services and operations.

The service builds on Sara, Seef's WhatsApp smart assistant, which is being integrated with AI agents developed with Total CX. The report names no malls and gives no launch date or usage figures.

Why it matters: A regional mall owner is making AI avatars and agents the first point of guest service, with a handover to staff, a model MECSC members can compare with their own customer-service desks.

BCG: 42% of large retailers let any AI shopping agent find their products, 32% admit only chosen agents and 6% block them

AI & techGlobalMarket data21 Oct 2026 · recentConfidence: High

A BCG report dated 21 Oct, based on a survey of 50 of the top 300 global retailers and 100 top global brands plus interviews, finds that 42% of retailers let any agent discover and retrieve their products, 32% give access only to chosen agents through direct data feeds, 6% block agents and 20% are still deciding; 87% never share customer data with AI agents.

Only 18% are deploying agentic commerce at scale, and 18% consider their marketplaces ready for agents. On loyalty, 14% plan to give external agents the same benefits as their own channels and 58% would offer a lower tier, while just 4% of consumers would let an agent complete a purchase on its own.

Why it matters: It gives Gulf retailers and mall operators a benchmark for deciding whether to open, limit or block AI shopping agents; no GCC retailer had published such a policy in the sources checked by 28 Oct.

Forrester's 2027 predictions: CX staffing to rise 20% after 'overzealous cuts', and B2C leaders to scale back a third of AI initiatives on cost

AI & techGlobalMarket data21 Oct 2026 · recentConfidence: High

Forrester's 2027 predictions for B2C marketing, CX and digital, released on 21 Oct, say rising AI costs will force B2C marketing leaders to scale back a third of their AI initiatives, because usage-based pricing turns every agent interaction, generated asset and automated workflow into a billable event.

Forrester predicts CX staffing will rise by 20% after 'overzealous cuts', with hiring focused on roles that support AI, such as NLP engineers, data scientists and AI experience designers. It also expects human-generated content to become a top priority again for 50% of brands, and one in 20 US and UK consumers to abandon traditional shopping for conversations with AI.

Why it matters: For MECSC's customer-service community, a leading analyst firm expects CX teams to grow again in 2027 around people who build and run AI, while AI budgets face tighter cost control.
