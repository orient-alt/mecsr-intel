Dubai Duty Free and Emirates strike exclusive passenger-benefits agreement

DestinationsGlobal14 Sep 2026 · recent

Dubai Duty Free (DDF) and Emirates formalised an exclusive partnership offering special discounts to Emirates passengers travelling through or to Dubai, focused on enhancing the airport shopping experience at DDF's Terminal 3 retail locations at Dubai International Airport. Senior executives from both organisations signed the agreement, which both parties described as "a shared commitment... to enhance the passenger experience on the ground, support Dubai's tourism and aviation sector, and grow traffic to and through Dubai across the Emirates network." Framed as a commercial loyalty-style tie-up rather than a new store format or renovation, but it's a clean, dated passenger-CX case study for members tracking how Gulf airports and their travel-retail partners are packaging experience benefits directly into the airline relationship rather than relying on walk-in footfall alone.

Knowledge Economic City accelerates five-district Madinah masterplan into delivery

MembersSaudi ArabiaNew property4 Sep 2026 · recentMECSR member

Knowledge Economic City (member) is advancing its 6.8 million sqm Madinah masterplan, designed for 200,000 residents and 42,000 hotel rooms, across five complementary districts. CEO Dr Moath Al-Yahya described the company as "a destination developer building a city within a city." Al Alyaa, the residential community, has reached 100% structural completion on select buildings with 1,490 premium units on sale; Madinah Gate, the transit-oriented district linked to the Haramain High-Speed Railway, now has its DoubleTree by Hilton hotel open; the 980,000 sqm Islamic World District (targeting 20,000 hotel rooms) has Clusters 1 and 2 under construction; and Multaqa AlMadinah, a leisure-destination partnership with Alrashid Properties, is advancing with multiple hotel brands. KEC's pipeline now exceeds 11,000 hotel rooms with Hilton, Marriott, Hyatt and Radisson.

DOO's Arabic-dialect-native AI customer experience platform gains GCC traction

AI & techGCCAI / tech24 Jun 2026 · recent

Bahrain-based AI platform DOO continues to scale its Arabic-first approach to enterprise customer experience, built around its proprietary OMLAQ engine, which is designed to understand and respond in regional dialects, Bahraini, Saudi, Kuwaiti, Emirati and others, rather than delivering mechanically translated responses. Beyond conversation, the platform offers multi-channel integration (WhatsApp, Instagram, websites), sentiment analysis via its Pulse engine, and workflow automation including customer categorisation and ticket routing. Since founding in 2024, DOO reports serving over 60 enterprise customers across telecommunications, airlines, banking and e-commerce in four GCC countries, growing ARR by 1,200%, securing $1.7M in seed funding, and signing a $2M+ partnership with Total CX. This is a third independent data point, after stc Bahrain's Bayan and Kuwait's Warba Bank "Bdr AI" (both previously logged), that dialect-native fluency specifically, not generic Arabic-language support, is becoming an explicit vendor-selection criterion for GCC enterprises deploying conversational AI.

MEBIS 2026 banking-innovation summit convenes in Dubai

AI & techSaudi ArabiaAI / tech2 Sep 2026 · recent

The Middle East Banking Innovation Summit (MEBIS) 2026 ran 16–17 September at Jumeirah Emirates Towers, Dubai, with more than 500 pre-registrations and banking leaders from the UAE, Saudi Arabia, Oman, Bahrain, Kuwait, Qatar, Egypt and Jordan expected to attend. The summit's central theme was the shift from generative AI assistants to agentic AI systems capable of executing tasks independently across platforms, a shift organisers framed as raising new governance and accountability questions rather than being purely a capability upgrade. Sessions also covered infrastructure modernisation, real-time/cross-border payments, data-governance strategy, and, directly relevant to members, balancing automation with personalisation, security and trust in digital banking customer experience. The accountability framing here is worth reading alongside this run's Global Trends Note: global contact-centre-AI commentary from the same week is making an almost identical argument (deployment volume no longer proves value; resolution quality and governance do), a sign the regional banking-AI conversation and the global CX-AI conversation are converging on the same maturity-stage concern at the same time.
